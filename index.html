Why do employees leave, and what does it cost?

HR Attrition & Cost AnalysisPower BIDAXExcel
Live report Code on GitHub LinkedIn post

What the data showed

  • 30.5% vs 10.4%Attrition for people working overtime against people who don't. Overtime staff are nearly 3 times as likely to leave.
  • $6.8MEstimated cost of 237 leavers out of 1,470 employees (16%), about $28.7K per leaver.
  • 40%Attrition among Sales Representatives (33 of 83), the highest of any role. Laboratory Technicians follow at 24%.
  • 112Leavers aged 25 to 34, the largest age group. Attrition is also highest in the first year at the company (36%).
  • $2.56MCost attached to Sales Executives alone. Their rate is a moderate 17%, but the group is large, so the money lost is the highest of any role.

What I would do

  1. Rebalance workload in the departments with heavy overtime to reduce burnout.
  2. Add mentoring and clear career steps for employees aged 25 to 34.
  3. Review pay and working conditions for Sales Representatives and Laboratory Technicians first.

How it was built: data cleaning and modeling, custom DAX for Attrition Rate, Estimated Attrition Cost and Cost per Employee, custom sort order for age brackets, dark-themed UI with page navigation and slicers.

Which products look strong on sales but lose margin?

Executive Sales & Profit OverviewPower BIDAXPower Query
Live report Code on GitHub LinkedIn post

What the data showed

  • $2M · 12.5%Total sales and overall profit margin ($286K profit). The Consumer segment brings in $1.16M of the $2.3M.
  • 17.4% / 17.0%Margins for Technology and Office Supplies, healthy in every segment.
  • 1.8%Furniture margin in the Consumer segment. Furniture sells $742K but earns only $18K profit.

How it was built: Power Query cleaning, DAX measures written so profit margin stays correct in a single Category by Segment cell and in the grand total, a combo chart of discount against profit, and waterfall charts for KPI tracking.

Why do loyal customers leave?

Customer Churn InsightsPower BIDAXPower Query
Live report Code on GitHub LinkedIn post

What the data showed

  • 14%Overall churn: 483 of 3,333 customers left, taking about $31.6K in revenue with them.
  • 46%Churn at 4 service calls, against 10 to 13% at 0 to 3 calls. It reaches 61% at 5 calls. Customers who stayed averaged 1.45 calls, those who left 2.23.
  • 17%Churn among customers with the longest tenure (15 to 19), up from 13% for the newest group. Long-time customers are not safe.
  • 26%Churn in California and New Jersey, the highest of any state. Texas follows at 25%.

What I would do

  1. Set an automatic alert when an account reaches 3 support calls, so the team acts before the 4-call jump.
  2. Review pricing and service for the longest-standing customers.
  3. Study service quality and competition in California and New Jersey.

How it was built: Power Query cleaning, DAX for churn rate and lost revenue, conditional formatting, and cross-filtering between visuals on a two-page report.
